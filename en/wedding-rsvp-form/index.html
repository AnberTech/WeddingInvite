Wedding RSVP form: a practical guide

Your headcount drives tables, catering, and budget. Here's how to set up your wedding RSVP form, pick a reply-by date, and collect every answer.

What does RSVP mean?

RSVP comes from the French phrase “répondez s'il vous plaît,” which means “please respond.” On a wedding invitation it's a simple request: let the couple know whether you're coming by a certain date. For a long time that meant mailing back a reply card in a stamped envelope. Today a digital RSVP form that collects replies in one list can do the same job in seconds, instead of a stack of cards, scattered texts, and notes from phone calls. It also spares guests a trip to the mailbox.

Why RSVPs matter so much

A lot of your plan depends on the headcount: tables and chairs, catering, the seating chart, even transportation. Venues and caterers usually ask for a final count some time before the wedding; check your own contract for the exact deadline. Guessing the number leads to either empty tables or not enough food. Collecting answers early takes the pressure off the last few weeks and lets you build your seating chart knowing exactly who will be there. It also lets you lock in your budget based on real numbers instead of estimates.

What to ask on your RSVP form

A good RSVP form is short; guests should be able to finish it in under a minute. The core questions: full name, whether they're attending, and how many people are coming. An optional note field lets guests mention anything special or leave a congratulations message. The RSVP form in a WeddingInvite invitation asks exactly that: full name, “Joyfully accept” or “Regretfully decline,” a party size from 1 to 10, and an optional note. Every extra question makes it a bit more likely that someone gives up halfway through, so keep it lean.

Setting a reply-by date

A safe approach is to set your RSVP deadline one to two weeks before the date your venue or caterer needs the final count, so you have time for late replies and reminders. Put the date on the invitation in plain words, such as: “Kindly reply by May 15.” A specific day works far better than vague phrasing like “as soon as possible.” If you send invitations six to eight weeks out, a deadline three to four weeks before the wedding works for most plans and still leaves room to finish the seating chart.

Following up and planning your headcount

Every wedding has guests who forget to reply, and it's usually forgetfulness rather than indifference. A short, friendly message a few days before the deadline, with the invitation link included, is often enough. To get your headcount, add up the party size from each yes and keep the declines on a separate list. Calling anyone who still hasn't answered after the deadline shrinks the unknowns. When you give the venue your final number, ask whether they recommend leaving a small buffer for last-minute changes.

Frequently asked questions

What does RSVP stand for on a wedding invitation?

RSVP stands for “répondez s'il vous plaît,” French for “please respond.” When you see it on an invitation, the hosts are asking you to let them know whether you'll attend by the date they've given.

Do I need to RSVP if I'm not attending?

Yes. Declining is just as important as accepting; it lets the couple finalize their headcount and avoid paying for a seat that stays empty. A short, kind reply is all it takes.

Can guests change their RSVP later?

In the WeddingInvite live sample, a reply is remembered in that browser after it's saved, and a “Change my reply” option appears so guests can update their attendance or party size. Sample replies aren't sent to the couple. If plans change at the last minute, it's still kind to tell the couple directly.

How do you handle plus-ones on an RSVP form?

The simplest way is to ask for the number of guests as a choice on the form; in WeddingInvite, guests pick a number from 1 to 10. Making it clear in your message who the invitation includes, such as one person, a couple, or a family, avoids confusion.

How far before the wedding should the RSVP deadline be?

There's no fixed rule; the deciding factor is when your venue or caterer needs a final count. Setting your deadline one to two weeks before that date leaves room for late replies and reminders. Three to four weeks before the wedding is common.
