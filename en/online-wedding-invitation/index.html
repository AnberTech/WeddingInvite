How to create an online wedding invitation

A digital wedding invitation puts the date, the venue, directions, and the RSVP in one link your guests open on their phone. Here's what to include and when to send it.

What to include

A good invitation answers your guests' questions before they ask. The essentials are short: your names, who is hosting, the date and time, and the venue name and address. Then add a way to RSVP and a reply-by date, plus a dress code or a note about kids if you need one. On a phone screen, long paragraphs get lost, so give each piece of information its own space. In a WeddingInvite invitation, the details appear across seven cards: your names, the wording and families, the date, the venue, the RSVP form, the weather for the day, and a closing card.

Wedding invitation wording ideas

Your wording can be formal or relaxed; what matters is that it sounds like the two of you. A classic line: “Together with their families, Elif and Ege request the pleasure of your company as they celebrate their marriage.” A more casual one: “We're getting married, and we'd love for you to be there.” Keep it to two or three sentences, and let the date, time, and address live in their own spots instead of burying them in the text. Listing your parents' names is a traditional touch. When you're done, read it out loud: if it sounds natural, you're on the right track.

Think about the guest experience

The first few seconds matter. A page designed for a phone, with clear text and easy scrolling, works for guests of any age. A WeddingInvite invitation opens with a sealed marble door: guests sweep the leaves away with a finger, or blow on them after allowing microphone access, tap the seal, and the door opens. Music plays in the background, with a button to turn it off. A countdown at the bottom shows the days, hours, minutes, and seconds until the wedding, and the “Add to calendar” and “Get directions” buttons mean nobody has to hunt for details on the day.

When and how to send the link

Wedding invitations typically go out six to eight weeks before the big day, and many couples send save-the-dates months earlier, especially for a destination wedding or lots of out-of-town guests. Sending the link in a personal message instead of a big group chat feels more thoughtful and makes guests more likely to reply. Write a short line of your own and put the link below it. About a week before the wedding, share the same link again as a friendly reminder, so the time, address, and directions are one tap away for everyone.

Pairing it with paper invitations

Going digital doesn't mean giving up paper. Plenty of couples mail printed invitations to grandparents and close family and send the link to everyone else. Printing a QR code that points to your online invitation on the card gives you the best of both: guests holding the card can scan it to reach directions, the calendar, and the RSVP form. That also lets you keep the printed design clean and simple, without cramming every detail onto it. Whatever mix you choose, double-check that the date, time, and address match exactly on both.

Frequently asked questions

Do guests need an app to open an online wedding invitation?

No. A WeddingInvite invitation is a single link that opens in the browser, with no app to download and no account to create. It's designed for a phone held upright; on a wide computer screen it opens in a phone-shaped frame.

Is it okay to send wedding invitations by text or email?

Yes. For most guests, a well-designed link sent with a personal note is both polite and practical. If some relatives would rather have something to hold, mail them a printed card and send the link as well.

How far in advance should I send an online wedding invitation?

Six to eight weeks before the wedding is the usual guideline. For out-of-town guests or a destination wedding, send a save-the-date earlier, and consider a short reminder about a week before the day.

Can I personalize the invitation for each guest?

Yes. Add ?k=Name to the end of the link and the first card greets the guest with “Dear Name,” while the RSVP form opens with their name already filled in. Just send each guest the link that ends with their own name.

How does the weather card work?

The weather card shows your wedding day. Within 14 days of the date it tries to load a forecast for that day; before that, it shows what was measured on the same date in a past year. If no data loads, the card keeps typical seasonal values and says it isn't a forecast. The data comes from Open-Meteo.
